1 · The error

The warrant that spelled out the truth

The platform says Rhodes hospital paid TAKEDA €18,590,421. The scanned warrant, same protocol number, writes the amount in words:

"# 185,904.21 # — one hundred eighty-five thousand nine hundred four euros and twenty-one cents"

Someone typed the amount without the decimal — one hundred times over. The same pattern appears 26 times nationally, up to €235M on a single invoice. ΑΔΑ: ΨΤ6Τ46907Κ-8ΕΘ

The paper always held the right answer — nobody was looking.

4 · The scale

"…and 148 others"

The largest "warrant" in the sample: €172 million at Σωτηρία hospital, payable to one named individual "and 148 others". It was not a purchase — it was hundreds of salaries compressed into a single record, leaving one employee's name as the hospital's largest apparent "supplier". In our own first pass, this artifact made Σωτηρία look suspiciously concentrated — we corrected it publicly.

Without document-level checking, even the auditor produces false accusations.

5 · The market

The discount that vanished in nine months

February 2022: Rhodes buys KEYTRUDA at €1,883 per box — 19% below the official hospital price of €2,332. November 2022: same drug, same hospital, €2,216. The contract itself explains why, verbatim:

"…amended by the company's document no. 18641/29-08-2022 regarding a change of commercial pricing policy for the product in question"

Across the 100 contracts we parsed, patented drugs sell at the tariff; discounts exist only where biosimilars compete. ΑΔΑ: Ψ9Π346907Κ-ΣΟΚ · Ψ4ΛΝ46907Κ-ΝΔΨ

Volume buys nothing. Competition buys everything.

6 · The tender

"The following offer was submitted" — singular

When Rhodes' international security tender stalled in competitors' appeals, the hospital ran a simplified procedure. The committee's minutes record the outcome in one revealing singular: the following offer was submitted — one. The company that submitted it won; in 2026, in another tender, it was again the only bidder. ΑΔΑ: 9ΕΩΠ46907Κ-4ΥΨ

Formally competitive; effectively a one-player market. The problem isn't the hospital — it's the thin market.

7 · The "supplier"

The company that didn't exist

For years, Rhodes' second-largest "supplier" was called "TECHNICAL SUPPORT OF RHODES HOSPITAL" — €2.15M in recurring payments just under the direct-award ceiling. It looked like textbook bid-splitting. The warrants revealed the truth: it is not a company — it is the accounting system's label for the electromechanical-maintenance consortium, under a properly tendered contract. ΑΔΑ: ΨΦΓΣ46907Κ-ΛΗΧ

Payment data alone would have convicted the innocent. The documents acquitted.

8 · The acquittal

The hospital that simply spent little

Κέρκυρα had everything a concealment case needs: last place in data quality, 40% below peers in spend per bed, shrinking records. Its published annual accounts — confirmed to the cent by a second document — showed the low numbers are real: the hospital genuinely spends little. Its bad score is sloppy field completion, not hidden money. ΑΔΑ: ΨΝΣΗ4690Β3-7Β0

The same method that detects, acquits. That is what makes it credible.
